\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Most of what ML-DSA adds to a C2PA credential sits in its certificates, not
its signature. An ML-DSA-65 manifest is \NvsESDSAsixtyfive{} times a
matched ES256 manifest, \NdeltaESDSAsixtyfive\,kB more per signer, and
certificates are \NchainShareManDSAsixtyfive\% of it; a timestamp brings a
second chain of the same size. Substituted into the production files we
collected, that is an estimated median \NcpqAddMedkB\,kB, or \NcpqPctMed\% of
the original file, and more than a tenth for the small images that services
sign at volume, which make up most of the quarter of our sample that grows
that much. Time is not the obstacle on application processors, and on
microcontrollers the carriage form sets the memory a verifier needs and the
energy its radio spends.

A substantial part of that cost can be recovered with no new mechanism.
Because the C2PA trust lists anchor issuing CAs, a signer and a time-stamping
authority whose issuers are listed can each carry their leaf alone, which the
reference implementation accepts and which takes \NtsBothLeafPct\% off a
timestamped manifest and halves each path a validator checks. It rests on a
reading of the specification we have asked its editors to confirm, and where it
requires listing an intermediate it changes the trust policy as well as the
bytes. A verifier that caches validated signers must key the cache on the
trust list, the policy, the validation time and the revocation status it
relied on, or it keeps accepting what it should reject.

The migration also tests the validators. Exercising the reference
implementation against post-quantum and classical credentials showed three
defects: time-stamping and OCSP-signing certificates accepted as claim
signers, a credential invalidated over a timestamp it should ignore, and
revocation stapled in a CA-signed OCSP response going unheeded. The last two
are fixed in the 0.92.0 release candidate; the first persists there. Each property a credential rests on
fails with a different classical signature: its signature and issuers protect
who signed,
its time-stamping authority when, and its revocation evidence whether the
signer was still trusted, and none of it holds while validators accept
classical credentials in the signer's name. The order of migration therefore
matters as much as its cost.
